The big picture

AI ⊃ ML ⊃ DL

AIArtificial Intelligence

Any technique that lets a computer do things we'd call intelligent: planning, reasoning, perceiving, talking. Includes hand-written rules and search, not just learning.

MLMachine Learning

Instead of writing the rules, we show examples and let an algorithm learn the rules from data. Decision trees, SVMs, linear regression and neural nets all live here.

DLDeep Learning

Machine learning with many-layered neural networks. Each layer learns features built from the layer before: edges → shapes → objects. It powers vision, speech and large language models.

How we got here

A short history of neural nets

1943

McCulloch & Pitts publish the first mathematical model of a neuron.

1958

Rosenblatt's Perceptron: a neuron that learns its own weights from data.

1986

Rumelhart, Hinton & Williams popularise backpropagation for multi-layer networks.

1998

LeCun's LeNet-5 uses convolutional networks to read handwritten digits.

2012

AlexNet wins ImageNet by a huge margin on GPUs. The deep learning boom begins.

2014

Generative Adversarial Networks: two networks compete to generate realistic images.

2017

"Attention Is All You Need" introduces the Transformer architecture.

2020s

Large language models and multimodal AI scale Transformers to billions of parameters.

01 · The building block

The Artificial Neuron

A neuron multiplies each input by a weight, adds them up with a bias, then passes the sum through an activation function. That's it. Everything else is this, repeated millions of times.

Cyan connections are positive weights (excite), pink are negative (inhibit). Thickness shows weight magnitude, and pulse size shows how much each input actually contributes (w·x).

06 · The engine of LLMs

Self-Attention

In a Transformer, every token computes how much to attend to every other token: softmax(Q·Kᵀ / √d). Different heads learn different relationships. Hover a token on the top row to see where it looks.
